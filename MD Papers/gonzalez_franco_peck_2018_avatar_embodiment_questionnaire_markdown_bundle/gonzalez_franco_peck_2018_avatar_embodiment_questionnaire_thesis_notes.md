# Thesis notes — Gonzalez-Franco & Peck 2018, “Avatar Embodiment. Towards a Standardized Questionnaire”

## Citation identity

Gonzalez-Franco, M., & Peck, T. C. (2018). *Avatar Embodiment. Towards a Standardized Questionnaire*. Frontiers in Robotics and AI, 5, Article 74. https://doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2018.00074

## What this paper is

This is a review and questionnaire-proposal paper for avatar embodiment in virtual reality. It does not study rowing, cycling, exergames, or XR sport directly. Its main contribution is practical: it reviews embodiment questionnaires used in prior VR and body-illusion experiments and proposes a standardized 25-item questionnaire for future avatar embodiment studies.

For the XR rowing thesis, this paper is useful as a methods/evaluation source rather than as the main theoretical definition of embodiment. Kilteni, Groten, and Slater (2012) should remain the conceptual anchor for sense of embodiment, while Gonzalez-Franco and Peck (2018) are useful for questionnaire construction and item wording.

## Content summary

The paper starts from the problem that in immersive VR, the user's real body is hidden by the headset. A self-avatar can replace the missing visual body by showing a collocated virtual body from the user's first-person perspective. When this works, the user may experience an embodiment illusion: the feeling that the co-located self-avatar has replaced their body at a physical and functional level.

The authors describe embodiment as a complex construct. It is not only body ownership. It also includes agency, co-location/body location, tactile sensations, external appearance, and response to external stimuli. They argue that self-avatars matter because having a body in VR affects perception, cognition, social interaction, behavior, presence, and plausibility.

The paper reviews more than 30 embodiment experiments published between 1998 and 2017 that cited the original Rubber Hand Illusion work and used virtual reality to induce embodiment. The authors classify the questionnaire items used in those studies into six categories:

1. Body ownership
   - whether the user felt the virtual body or body part was their own.

2. Agency and motor control
   - whether the user felt they controlled the virtual body and whether the avatar's movements were caused by their own movements.

3. Tactile sensations
   - whether touch or haptic stimulation felt located on or caused by the virtual body.

4. Location of the body
   - whether the user felt located where the virtual body was, or whether their body drifted toward the virtual body.

5. External appearance
   - whether the avatar's shape, clothing, posture, skin tone, or other visual features felt related to the user's body.

6. Response to external stimuli
   - whether events affecting the virtual body, such as touch or threat, felt as if they could affect the user's own body.

The authors then propose a 25-item questionnaire. They recommend using a 7-point Likert scale from strongly disagree (-3) to strongly agree (+3). They also propose possible aggregation formulas and suggest weighting ownership, agency, and self-location more strongly because these are central aspects of embodiment.

The paper is careful about limitations. The proposed questionnaire is based on prevalent items from prior studies, but it is not presented as a fully psychometrically validated gold-standard instrument. The authors describe it as a standardized, backwards-compatible proposal that future researchers can use and refine.

## Why it matters for the XR rowing thesis

This paper matters because the thesis likely needs to measure embodiment in two avatar-representation modes:

1. Erg-centered / high-congruence mode
   - the avatar hands/body/handle are mapped closely to the real erg handle, seat, and user motion.

2. Boat-centered / sport-depiction mode
   - the avatar appears to row in a boat with oars, oarlocks, blades, and on-water rowing logic, even if the virtual oar/hand motion does not perfectly match the real erg handle path.

Gonzalez-Franco and Peck provide item categories that map directly to this comparison:
- ownership: did the virtual rowing body/hands feel like mine?
- agency: did the avatar's movement feel caused by my movement?
- location: did my body feel located where I saw the virtual body?
- appearance: did the avatar body look/feel appropriate or self-like?
- response/tactile: optional, if threat/touch/handle contact is included.

This lets the thesis avoid treating embodiment as one vague score. It can instead compare subcomponents: maybe the erg-centered mode scores higher in agency/location, while the boat-centered mode scores higher in sport-action plausibility or appearance-related realism. That distinction is central to the thesis.

## Core findings / cheat sheet

- A self-avatar is a collocated avatar shown from first-person perspective that can replicate the user's posture and movements.
- An embodiment illusion occurs when the user feels that this self-avatar has replaced their body at a physical and functional level.
- Embodiment is complex and includes body ownership, agency, body location/co-location, tactile sensations, external appearance, and response to external stimuli.
- Body ownership is the most common questionnaire category in prior embodiment studies.
- Agency questions became more common as body tracking and visuomotor stimulation became more common.
- Synchronous visuomotor correlations can strongly enhance embodiment and may be stronger than visuotactile stimulation alone for some full-body setups.
- Co-location of avatar and user is critical for embodiment.
- Avatar appearance can enhance or inhibit embodiment, but embodiment is possible even when avatars differ from the user's real age, size, gender, race, or body shape.
- Questionnaires remain common because they are easy to administer, even though subjective questionnaires have limitations.
- The proposed standardized questionnaire has 25 items grouped into six categories.
- The authors recommend randomizing question order and using a 7-point Likert scale.
- The questionnaire is a proposal based on prior literature, not a final universally validated measurement instrument.
- Embodiment, presence, and immersion are related but should not be collapsed.

## Relation to thesis concepts

### Sense of embodiment

Very relevant. This paper operationalizes sense of embodiment for VR avatar studies. It is best used together with Kilteni et al. (2012), where Kilteni provides the conceptual structure and Gonzalez-Franco/Peck provide questionnaire wording.

### Body ownership

High relevance. The ownership item “I felt as if the virtual ____ was my ____” is directly useful for a rowing avatar or virtual hands/arms.

Possible adapted item:
- I felt as if the virtual rowing body was my body.
- I felt as if the virtual hands on the handle were my hands.

### Sense of agency

High relevance. Agency items are central for the thesis because the two rowing modes likely differ in how clearly the avatar's movement is caused by the user's own movement.

Possible adapted items:
- It felt like I could control the virtual rowing body as if it was my own body.
- The movements of the virtual rower were caused by my movements.
- The movements of the virtual hands/arms were caused by my movements.

### Self-location / body location

High relevance. Co-location is central to the erg-centered mode. If the virtual handle/hands/body align with the real erg and user's felt body, body-location ratings may be higher.

Possible adapted item:
- I felt as if my body was located where I saw the virtual rowing body.

### Visuomotor congruence

Highly relevant indirectly. The questionnaire does not use the word “visuomotor congruence” as the thesis does, but agency and motor-control items capture the user's experience of movement matching and control.

### Visuotactile / haptic congruence

Relevant if the real erg handle is visually represented as touched by virtual hands. Tactile items may be adapted carefully, but only if the study uses explicit touch/haptic events. Otherwise, do not overload the questionnaire with irrelevant tactile items.

Possible adapted handle-contact item:
- It seemed as if the touch I felt on the real handle was located where I saw the virtual hands/handle contact.

Use only if that construct is actually important.

### Morphological similarity / avatar realism

Medium relevance. The appearance questions can help if avatar body proportions, hands, or rower body shape differ across conditions or are customized. If the avatar is the same in both modes, appearance items may be less central.

### Presence / Plausibility

Relevant but separate. The paper discusses how embodiment may increase presence, place illusion, and plausibility, but these are not the same construct. This is useful for writing carefully.

### Rowing biomechanics

Not relevant directly. This paper is not about rowing technique.

### Vection

Not relevant. Vection should stay separate from embodiment.

## How it maps to my thesis modes

### Erg-centered mode

This paper strongly supports evaluating the erg-centered mode through ownership, agency, and body-location items. The erg-centered condition is designed to maximize:
- real-to-virtual handle alignment,
- hand/body movement correspondence,
- physical and virtual co-location,
- haptic/proprioceptive consistency,
- action-outcome clarity.

Therefore, the most important questionnaire categories for this mode are:
- body ownership,
- agency/motor control,
- body location.

A likely hypothesis or interpretation could be:
- The erg-centered mode may produce stronger agency and body-location ratings because the virtual body follows the real erg handle/seat/user motion more directly.

### Boat-centered mode

This paper also supports evaluating the boat-centered mode. The boat-centered mode may look more sport-realistic, but may introduce movement mismatch because virtual oar/hand trajectories differ from the real erg handle trajectory.

The questionnaire can reveal whether that mismatch affects:
- ownership,
- agency,
- location,
- perceived control.

However, the questionnaire does not directly measure sport-action congruence. A separate custom item may be needed for that.

Possible custom sport-action item:
- The virtual rowing movement looked like realistic on-water rowing.
- The rowing action I saw matched my expectation of real rowing.

### Core tradeoff

This paper is useful because it allows the thesis to split the embodiment tradeoff into measurable subcomponents.

Instead of asking only:
- Which mode was more embodied?

The thesis can ask:
- Which mode felt more owned?
- Which mode felt more controllable?
- Which mode felt more co-located?
- Which mode looked more like rowing?
- Which mode made the physical/virtual mismatch more noticeable?

That is exactly the kind of structure the thesis needs.

## What not to overclaim

- Do not claim this paper proves embodiment improves rowing.
- Do not claim this paper proves the proposed questionnaire is a fully validated gold-standard scale.
- Do not claim all 25 items must be used in the thesis.
- Do not claim embodiment is only questionnaire-based.
- Do not collapse embodiment, presence, and immersion.
- Do not use tactile/threat items if the rowing study does not include tactile/threat events.
- Do not use appearance items as a proxy for ownership unless appearance is actually manipulated.
- Do not treat total embodiment score as more informative than subscales without justification.

## Candidate thesis paragraph

Gonzalez-Franco and Peck propose a standardized questionnaire for avatar embodiment in VR based on questionnaire items used in more than 30 prior embodiment studies. They classify embodiment-related items into body ownership, agency and motor control, tactile sensations, body location, external appearance, and response to external stimuli. This structure is useful for the present XR rowing study because the comparison between erg-centered and boat-centered representations should not treat embodiment as a single undifferentiated construct. A high-congruence erg-centered avatar may support agency and body-location through close matching of real and virtual handle, seat, and body movement, while a boat-centered avatar may support sport-action plausibility but introduce movement mismatch. Separating ownership, agency, and location items therefore allows the evaluation to describe which aspect of embodiment is affected by the representation mode.

## Candidate short thesis sentence

Gonzalez-Franco and Peck's standardized questionnaire proposal is useful for operationalizing avatar embodiment in XR rowing because it separates ownership, agency, body location, appearance, tactile sensation, and response-to-stimuli items.

## Candidate glossary wording

### Avatar embodiment questionnaire

An avatar embodiment questionnaire measures how strongly users experience a virtual body as their own body. Gonzalez-Franco and Peck propose grouping such questions into ownership, agency, body location, tactile sensation, external appearance, and response to external stimuli. For XR rowing, the most relevant dimensions are ownership, agency, and body location, because the thesis compares whether a virtual rower feels more embodied when it closely matches the real erg movement or when it depicts more realistic on-water rowing.

### Ownership, agency, and location in XR rowing

Ownership concerns whether the virtual body or hands feel like mine. Agency concerns whether the virtual rower’s movement feels caused and controlled by my own movement. Location concerns whether my felt body is where I see the virtual body. These dimensions should be separated because a rowing representation can look sport-realistic while still weakening agency or co-location if the virtual oar/hand movement no longer matches the real erg handle trajectory.

## Codex / agent note

Use this paper as the practical questionnaire source for avatar embodiment. Do not treat it as the main conceptual source; pair it with Kilteni et al. (2012). For the rowing thesis, adapt only the relevant categories, especially ownership, agency, and location. Consider appearance only if avatar visual realism/customization is discussed, and tactile items only if handle contact or touch congruence is explicitly part of the evaluation.

Recommended adapted items:
- I felt as if the virtual rowing body was my body.
- I felt as if the virtual hands were my hands.
- It felt like I could control the virtual rowing body as if it was my own body.
- The movements of the virtual rowing body were caused by my movements.
- The movements of the virtual hands/arms were caused by my movements.
- I felt as if my body was located where I saw the virtual rowing body.
- I felt as if my hands were located where I saw the virtual hands.

Add custom sport-action congruence items separately; this questionnaire does not measure that directly.
