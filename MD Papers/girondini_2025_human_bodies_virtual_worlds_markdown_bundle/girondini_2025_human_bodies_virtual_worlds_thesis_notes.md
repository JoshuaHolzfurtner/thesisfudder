## Bibliographic anchor

Girondini, M., Mariano, M., Stanco, G., Gallace, A., & Zapparoli, L. (2025). *Human bodies in virtual worlds: a systematic review of implicit sense of agency and ownership measured in immersive virtual reality environments*. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience, 19, 1553574. https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2025.1553574

## Why this paper is useful for the thesis

This is a recent systematic review focused on **immersive VR** studies of sense of agency (SoA) and sense of body ownership (SoO), specifically where studies include **implicit measures** such as intentional binding, proprioceptive drift, and skin conductance response. It is useful as a bridge between older embodiment foundations such as Kilteni et al. (2012) and the current state of VR-specific experimental manipulation.

For a VR rowing thesis, it is especially relevant because it separates different manipulation families that map well onto the rowing implementation question:

- **Agency manipulations:** real-body/virtual-body action-outcome congruence, especially visuomotor congruence and movement control.
- **Ownership manipulations:** physical congruence, spatial congruence, and stimulation congruence between real and virtual body/limb.
- **Measurement issue:** implicit and explicit measures often diverge, so questionnaires alone are not the whole story.

## Core concepts

### Sense of agency (SoA)

The paper treats SoA as the feeling of controlling actions and, through them, events in the external world. In VR, this is tied strongly to the spatiotemporal coherence between intended real movements and virtual body/action outcomes.

**Thesis relevance:** In an erg-to-avatar rowing setup, SoA is likely most threatened by mismatches between the real handle/seat/body movement and the virtual rowing movement, especially timing, spatial trajectory, and kinematic congruence.

### Sense of body ownership (SoO)

The paper treats SoO as the feeling that the body is one's own. It distinguishes body ownership from more general ownership of objects/tools.

**Thesis relevance:** A realistic rower avatar or boat-body depiction may help body ownership through human-likeness, anatomical plausibility, and spatial alignment, but physical realism alone may not be enough if sensorimotor or multisensory congruence is poor.

### Implicit vs explicit measures

The paper emphasizes that explicit self-report measures and implicit measures may capture different underlying processes. Examples:

- SoA implicit measure: **intentional binding**.
- SoO implicit measures: **proprioceptive drift** and **skin conductance response** to threat.

**Thesis relevance:** If the rowing evaluation uses only questionnaires, it should be framed as measuring explicit self-report. If using physiological threat/defense responses or body-position drift, those should be treated as complementary rather than interchangeable.

## Methodological scope of the review

The review searched PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science in May 2025. It included studies with immersive VR manipulations targeting SoA and/or SoO and using implicit outcome measures. It excluded studies relying only on self-report, mixed/augmented reality rather than fully immersive VR, and clinical/children-only samples.

The final dataset is reported as 34 papers / 47 experiments in the tables. The table pages are layout-heavy; verify any exact count or study categorization against the PDF tables.

## Main results to remember

### 1. Agency manipulations strongly affect implicit SoA

Agency manipulations, especially visuomotor congruence, had the clearest effect on implicit SoA. Reduced timing/spatial/kinematic congruence generally reduced implicit agency.

**Rowing translation:** Your erg-centered condition is theoretically strong for SoA if the virtual body responds in near-real time and follows the real user's movement closely. The boat-centered condition may still work, but the changed hand/oar trajectory is the risky variable.

### 2. Ownership manipulations affect implicit SoO, but unevenly

Ownership manipulations affected implicit SoO to different extents:

- spatial congruence: moderate effect,
- stimulation congruence: moderate effect,
- physical congruence: mild effect.

**Rowing translation:** Avatar realism helps, but spatial alignment and synchronized sensorimotor/multisensory cues may matter more than visual polish alone.

### 3. Ownership manipulations did not affect implicit SoA in the reviewed evidence

The review reports that changes in body appearance / physical congruence did not modulate implicit SoA in the included studies.

**Rowing translation:** A realistic rower body may not rescue agency if motion mapping feels off. Conversely, a less perfect avatar can still feel agentive if it moves exactly with the user.

### 4. Agency and ownership can be dissociated, but not totally isolated

The paper supports partial independence: SoA and SoO can be functionally dissociated in VR manipulations. However, interactions exist; visuomotor congruence can mildly affect implicit SoO.

**Rowing translation:** The thesis comparison should not present visuomotor congruence and realistic sport depiction as mutually exclusive magic switches. They interact, but the experiment can emphasize which dimension is prioritized.

### 5. Explicit and implicit measures often diverge

The review repeatedly warns that implicit and explicit measures show heterogeneous alignment. Explicit questionnaires may detect effects that implicit measures do not, and vice versa.

**Rowing translation:** If participants say the boat-centered depiction is more immersive or realistic, that does not automatically mean stronger implicit body ownership or agency. Likewise, physiological/behavioral measures may show something not obvious in self-report.

## Good ways to use this paper in the thesis

- To justify separating **agency** and **ownership** instead of treating embodiment as one undifferentiated score.
- To justify why **visuomotor congruence** is central to the erg-centered condition.
- To justify why **realistic visual depiction** is not sufficient by itself for agency.
- To motivate a careful distinction between **explicit questionnaires** and **implicit/physiological measures**.
- To support the idea that VR uniquely allows body/limb manipulations that are impossible or difficult in traditional lab settings.

## Do-not-overclaim warnings

- Do not claim that physical realism is irrelevant. The review says physical congruence has a mild effect on implicit SoO, not zero effect on everything.
- Do not claim that agency and ownership are fully independent. The review frames them as dissociable but dynamically interacting.
- Do not claim that implicit measures are automatically superior. The paper treats them as different and complementary, not as universally more truthful.
- Do not cite the review as evidence about rowing specifically. It is about immersive VR embodiment experiments, not rowing training.
- Be careful with the tables. The PDF's table extraction is layout-heavy; verify exact rows/counts in the original PDF.

## Possible thesis phrasing

> Girondini et al. (2025) review immersive VR studies that manipulate agency- and ownership-relevant features while measuring implicit outcomes such as intentional binding, proprioceptive drift, and skin conductance response. Their synthesis suggests that visuomotor congruence is especially influential for implicit agency, whereas ownership-related manipulations such as spatial and stimulation congruence more strongly affect implicit ownership. They also report heterogeneous agreement between implicit and explicit measures, supporting the use of multiple complementary measures when evaluating embodiment in VR.

## Connection to the rowing comparison

For the rowing thesis, this paper supports a clean theoretical framing:

- **Erg-centered mode:** likely stronger in visuomotor congruence and therefore agency, because the virtual handle/body movement can be mapped closely to the real erg movement.
- **Boat-centered mode:** likely stronger in sport depiction and rowing realism, but may introduce spatial/kinematic mismatches in hand/oar trajectories.
- **Evaluation implication:** compare not just “which feels more immersive,” but whether participants distinguish control/agency, ownership, self-location, realism, and sport plausibility.
