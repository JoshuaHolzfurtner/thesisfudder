# Thesis notes — Kalckert & Ehrsson (2012), “Moving a rubber hand that feels like your own: a dissociation of ownership and agency”

## Citation identity

Kalckert, A., & Ehrsson, H. H. (2012). Moving a rubber hand that feels like your own: a dissociation of ownership and agency. *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 6, 40.

## What this paper is

A “moving rubber hand illusion” paradigm where participants move (or have moved) their index finger while seeing a model/rubber hand’s index finger move.

Key manipulations:
- synchrony vs asynchrony between real and seen finger movement
- active vs passive movement generation (voluntary vs externally moved)
- anatomically congruent vs incongruent hand position (rotated hand)

Measures:
- questionnaires for ownership and agency (with control items)
- proprioceptive drift pointing task (objective ownership proxy)

## Why it matters for your VR rowing thesis

This paper is one of the cleanest experimental demonstrations that:
- ownership and agency are distinct and can be dissociated (double dissociation)
- both are highly sensitive to temporal congruence (synchrony)
- agency can exist without ownership (agency over “external object” actions)
- ownership can exist without agency (passive movement with congruent body cues)

This supports your thesis structure of treating SoA vs SoO separately instead of collapsing them into “embodiment.”

## Core findings (the “cheat sheet”)

### 1) Synchrony is required for both ownership and agency
Asynchrony eliminated both ownership and agency (used as a control).

### 2) Passive movement abolishes agency but can leave ownership intact
When the participant’s finger is moved passively (no voluntary motor command), agency breaks down strongly, but ownership can remain if other cues support it.

### 3) Anatomical incongruence diminishes ownership but does not eliminate agency
Rotating the rubber/model hand into an implausible orientation reduced ownership, but agency ratings could still be affirmed—interpreted as agency over an external object/tool-like mapping.

### 4) Double dissociation + “body agency” vs “external agency”
They argue (carefully) that agency feels stronger when the limb is experienced as part of the body (body agency) versus when agency is experienced over an external object (external agency). They also found ownership–agency correlation only in the active + congruent condition (owned limb).

### 5) Proprioceptive drift tracks ownership, not agency
They report proprioceptive drift toward the rubber hand under ownership conditions and correlations between drift and ownership ratings, but not drift and agency.

## How to use it in your thesis

### As a foundational argument for your constructs
Use it to justify:
- separate measurement/reporting of agency vs ownership
- why visuomotor congruence (timing match) is crucial for both
- why anatomical plausibility/alignment matters more for ownership than for agency

### As a bridge to tool/oar agency in your boat mode
Their “external agency” idea is a neat fit:
- A user can feel agency over an action even if ownership of the moved/seen body part is weaker (similar to using tools)
- In your rowing boat mode, the oar/blade may be “tool-like,” but your goal is to push it toward “body-like” embodiment by using a coherent avatar and strong sensorimotor mapping.

## What not to overclaim

- This is a finger/hand paradigm, not whole-body VR rowing.
- Proprioceptive drift is debated as a sole ownership measure; use it as supporting evidence, not as the only proof.
- “Body agency vs external agency” is suggestive rather than conclusively proven; cite it as a discussion-level interpretation.
