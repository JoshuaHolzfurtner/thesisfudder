# Thesis notes — Seinfeld & Müller (2020), “Impact of visuomotor feedback on the embodiment of virtual hands detached from the body”

## Citation identity

Seinfeld, S., & Müller, J. (2020). Impact of visuomotor feedback on the embodiment of virtual hands detached from the body. *Scientific Reports*, 10, 22427.

## What this paper is

This paper investigates whether rich visuomotor feedback can preserve embodiment when users see virtual hands that are disconnected from a full virtual body.

Participants interacted in VR with either:
- connected virtual hands/forearms attached to a virtual body, or
- disconnected floating virtual hands.

The hands were controlled using real-time hand/finger tracking. The paper includes two experiments using bimanual target-touching tasks, questionnaire measures, and in Experiment 1 a preliminary virtual threat/SCR measure.

## Why it matters for the thesis

This is directly relevant to the rowing/XR project because it asks whether strong motor control can compensate for reduced body realism or body continuity.

It is especially useful as a counterbalance to Brugada-Ramentol et al. (2019). Brugada-Ramentol et al. found that active control could help maintain ownership over a detached virtual hand. Seinfeld & Müller find that even with rich visuomotor feedback, disconnected hands still produced lower ownership/control/self-location than connected hands.

This gives the thesis a more nuanced claim:

- Visuomotor congruence is important.
- Active control can support embodiment.
- But visual body continuity and morphological plausibility still matter.
- Full-body or connected-body representation may therefore be valuable even when hands are accurately tracked.

## Core findings

### 1. Disconnected hands reduced embodiment even with rich visuomotor feedback

Across two experiments, connected hands produced stronger self-reported body ownership, control, and self-location than disconnected hands.

This is important because the study used more than passive observation: participants actively controlled the hands and fingers in real time.

### 2. Agency was not clearly reduced in the same way

The paper reports that agency did not significantly differ between connected and disconnected hands in the same way as ownership/control/self-location.

Interpretation for thesis:
- A user can feel they control floating hands while still feeling weaker ownership or body-location congruence.
- Agency and ownership should therefore not be collapsed into one vague “embodiment” score.

### 3. Motor-performance findings were mixed

Experiment 1 suggested some performance differences, but Experiment 2 controlled task difficulty more carefully and did not replicate a clear motor-performance effect of connected versus disconnected hands.

Interpretation:
- Do not claim connected body representation reliably improves task performance based on this paper.
- Use it mainly for subjective embodiment/body-continuity claims.

### 4. Threat/SCR evidence was preliminary

Experiment 1 included a virtual shuriken threat and skin conductance recording, but only one threat trial per condition. The authors themselves treat those data cautiously.

Interpretation:
- Do not overuse the SCR result.
- It is acceptable to mention as preliminary evidence that visual continuity did not clearly modulate threat-related responses in this setup.

### 5. Many participants did not consciously notice the manipulation

A striking result: about half of participants did not explicitly report noticing that the forearms/body continuity differed between conditions, despite the visual difference being clear.

Interpretation:
- Embodiment and performance can be affected by body representation changes even when participants do not explicitly notice them.
- For your thesis methods, this supports checking whether participants notice differences between modes or avatar/body representations.

## Relation to the rowing/XR thesis

This paper supports the importance of rendering the user as more than disembodied hands/controllers.

Potential thesis argument:

“Although congruent visuomotor control can support embodiment, Seinfeld and Müller show that body continuity still contributes to ownership and self-location even when rich visuomotor feedback is available. This motivates the use of a coherent avatar representation rather than only floating hands/oars.”

For the boat-centered rowing mode:
- If the system shows a full avatar with connected arms and oars, that may support body continuity and self-location.
- If the system only showed floating hands/oars, it might preserve agency but weaken ownership/self-location.
- Therefore, full-body IK and connected avatar representation are not merely cosmetic; they are part of the embodiment design.

For the erg-centered mode:
- The argument becomes even stronger: precise handle/seat mapping plus coherent avatar body continuity may jointly support embodiment.

## How it balances Brugada-Ramentol et al. (2019)

Brugada-Ramentol:
- Active control can maintain or increase ownership for a detached virtual hand under some conditions.
- Noisy/incongruent movement reduces ownership and agency.

Seinfeld & Müller:
- Even with rich visuomotor feedback, disconnected hands still diminish embodiment compared with connected hands.
- Agency may remain high, but ownership/control/self-location are weaker.

Together:
- Visuomotor feedback is powerful but not all-powerful.
- Body continuity and visual plausibility still matter.
- Best thesis claim: embodiment depends on both bottom-up sensorimotor congruence and top-down body-form plausibility.

## What not to overclaim

Do not say detached hands cannot be embodied. The paper explicitly indicates that disconnected hands still produced relatively high embodiment/agency scores.

Do not claim motor performance is definitely worse with disconnected hands. The motor results were contradictory.

Do not treat the SCR/threat finding as strong evidence. It was preliminary and based on one threat trial per condition.

Do not directly generalize from virtual hands to full rowing avatars without caution. Use it as supporting evidence for body continuity and hand/arm representation, not as proof for rowing.
