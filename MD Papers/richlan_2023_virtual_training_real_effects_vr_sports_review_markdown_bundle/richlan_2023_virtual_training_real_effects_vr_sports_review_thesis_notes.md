# Thesis notes — Richlan et al. 2023, “Virtual training, real effects: a narrative review on sports performance enhancement through interventions in virtual reality”

## Citation identity

Richlan, F., Weiß, M., Kastner, P., & Braid, J. (2023). *Virtual training, real effects: a narrative review on sports performance enhancement through interventions in virtual reality*. Frontiers in Psychology, 14, 1240790. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1240790

## What this paper is

This is a narrative review of intervention studies using virtual reality in sports contexts. It reviews VR training studies with pre-post designs and discusses whether VR interventions can improve sports performance, support transfer to real-world performance, and provide useful training environments.

For the XR rowing thesis, this paper is not a rowing paper and not an embodiment paper in the strict sense. Its value is as a **VR sport transfer and fidelity source**. It helps articulate why “realism” in VR sport should not be treated as a single dimension. The key thesis-relevant vocabulary is:

- transfer,
- generalizability,
- validity,
- fidelity,
- physical fidelity,
- psychological fidelity,
- affective fidelity,
- biomechanical fidelity,
- adaptive difficulty,
- ecological validity,
- real-world transfer.

This is useful because the thesis compares two different kinds of realism/fidelity:

1. **Erg-centered / high-congruence mode**
   - higher fidelity to the user’s actual indoor-rowing action and physical device,
   - stronger body/device/handle congruence,
   - stronger action-response congruence.

2. **Boat-centered / sport-depiction mode**
   - higher visual/contextual/sport-action fidelity to on-water rowing,
   - more rowing-faithful boat/oar depiction,
   - but lower biomechanical/device fidelity to the real erg handle path.

## Why it matters for the XR rowing thesis

The thesis’s central conceptual risk is using the word “realistic” too broadly. Richlan et al. help avoid that. They review sport VR studies and discuss how VR transfer depends on whether virtual training environments resemble and function like real sports environments in relevant ways.

This matters because the boat-centered mode may look more realistic as rowing, while the erg-centered mode may feel more realistic as action. Richlan’s fidelity/validity vocabulary helps phrase this distinction:

- **sport-context fidelity**: the scene looks like rowing on water,
- **biomechanical/action fidelity**: the movement and feedback match the user’s actual body/device action,
- **psychological fidelity**: the environment evokes similar mental states or task demands,
- **transfer validity**: whether training effects or learned behavior carry into real-world performance.

The thesis does not evaluate training transfer, so Richlan should not be used as a main empirical training source. Instead, it helps frame the design rationale and the discussion:

> A virtual sport representation can be visually convincing but still not equivalent to real-world sport action if biomechanical, haptic, perceptual, or task constraints differ.

## Core findings / cheat sheet

- Richlan et al. reviewed VR/XR sport intervention studies with pre-post designs.
- The review included 12 intervention studies and an aggregated sample of N = 493 participants.
- The sports included target/precision sports, bat/racquet and ball sports, goal sports, martial arts, and sport-unspecific processes such as balance.
- Many studies found statistically significant effects in relevant target skills after VR intervention, often outperforming passive or active control conditions.
- VR may support training of:
  - motor skills,
  - perception-action skills,
  - strategic/tactical/decision-making skills,
  - psychological resilience or performance under pressure.
- The paper repeatedly emphasizes that real-world transfer and generalizability remain central issues.
- Transfer depends on how well the VR system represents relevant constraints of the real task.
- Fidelity and validity are key concepts for evaluating VR sport training.
- The review discusses different kinds of fidelity, including physical, psychological, affective, and biomechanical fidelity.
- VR training studies are promising but heterogeneous; the field still needs careful methodology and clearer evidence of transfer.

## Relation to thesis concepts

### VR sport training

High relevance for background, but not for the main embodiment hypothesis. Richlan shows that VR sport training has become an active research field, but the thesis should not drift into claiming that the prototype trains rowing technique.

### Transfer

High conceptual relevance. The thesis does not test transfer to on-water rowing, but the concept of transfer is useful in the limitations section. If the boat-centered mode looks realistic, that does not mean the system trains real rowing technique unless transfer is evaluated.

### Generalizability

Moderate relevance. The thesis can use generalizability as a caution: results from a prototype test on an ergometer may not automatically generalize to on-water rowing, rowing training, or long-term exercise adherence.

### Validity

High conceptual relevance. Richlan’s discussion of validity helps frame whether a VR sport task represents relevant real-world sport features. For XR rowing, the boat-centered mode may increase face/context validity as rowing, while the erg-centered mode may increase validity for the actual indoor-rowing interaction.

### Fidelity

Very high relevance. This is the most useful concept from the paper. The thesis can use the term carefully to distinguish:

- visual fidelity,
- physical fidelity,
- biomechanical fidelity,
- psychological fidelity,
- sport-action fidelity,
- haptic/proprioceptive fidelity.

### Biomechanical fidelity

Very high relevance to the erg-vs-boat comparison. The erg-centered mode may preserve biomechanical fidelity to the user’s actual erg motion, while the boat-centered mode may reduce it by depicting oar movement that does not match the real handle path.

### Embodiment

Indirect relevance. Richlan is not an embodiment scale or body-ownership source, but it supports the broader idea that representation quality and action fidelity matter in VR sport systems.

### Presence / plausibility

Indirect relevance. Use Slater and Skarbez for presence/plausibility theory. Use Richlan only for sport-training design and fidelity.

### Rowing

Low direct relevance. This paper is not rowing-specific. Use it for VR sport background and design vocabulary, not rowing technique.

## How it maps to my thesis modes

### Erg-centered mode

The erg-centered mode can be described as emphasizing **biomechanical/action fidelity to the actual indoor-rowing situation**. It maps the user’s real handle, seat, and body movement closely to the virtual avatar. This may support agency and ownership because the virtual movement corresponds directly to the user’s real action loop.

### Boat-centered mode

The boat-centered mode can be described as emphasizing **visual/contextual/sport-action fidelity to on-water rowing**. It may better represent rowing as a sport, including oars, boat movement, blades, and water context. However, it may reduce biomechanical/action fidelity because the real erg handle path and felt forces do not match the virtual oar path and blade-water interaction.

### Core tradeoff

Richlan helps phrase the thesis as a tradeoff between fidelity dimensions:

- Erg-centered mode: higher biomechanical/device congruence.
- Boat-centered mode: higher visual/contextual sport congruence.
- Research question: which fidelity dimension contributes more to virtual embodiment in XR rowing?

## Candidate thesis paragraph

VR sport literature often frames the usefulness of virtual training systems through validity, fidelity, and transfer. Richlan et al. review VR sport intervention studies and argue that real-world transfer and generalizability depend on how well the virtual environment represents the relevant features and constraints of the real task. This is useful for XR rowing because the visually most sport-faithful representation is not necessarily the most embodied one. A boat-centered representation may increase visual and contextual fidelity to on-water rowing, while an erg-centered representation may preserve biomechanical and action fidelity to the user’s actual indoor-rowing movement. The thesis therefore treats “realism” as multidimensional rather than assuming that more detailed sport depiction automatically produces stronger embodiment.

## Candidate short thesis sentence

Richlan et al.’s discussion of VR sport fidelity and transfer helps frame the thesis comparison as a tradeoff between biomechanical/action fidelity to the ergometer and visual/contextual fidelity to on-water rowing.

## Candidate implementation/design documentation note

Design documentation can cite Richlan et al. to justify separating different fidelity dimensions. The prototype deliberately implements two representations rather than one supposedly “most realistic” representation: an erg-centered mode that preserves the user’s actual action loop, and a boat-centered mode that represents the sport logic of on-water rowing. This avoids treating realism as a single visual axis.

## Candidate glossary wording

### Fidelity in VR sport

Fidelity describes the degree to which a virtual sport system preserves relevant features of the real sport task. This can include visual fidelity, physical fidelity, psychological fidelity, affective fidelity, and biomechanical fidelity. In XR rowing, a boat scene may have high visual/contextual fidelity to rowing, while an erg-centered avatar may have higher biomechanical fidelity to the user’s actual movement.

### Transfer

Transfer describes whether effects, skills, or behavior learned in VR carry over to real-world performance. The XR rowing thesis does not test transfer to on-water rowing, so it should not claim that either representation teaches rowing technique unless a separate transfer study is conducted.

### Biomechanical fidelity

Biomechanical fidelity refers to whether the movement demands and body-action constraints in VR correspond to those of the real task. In XR rowing, the erg-centered mode may have high biomechanical fidelity to the indoor-rowing device, while the boat-centered mode may have lower biomechanical fidelity to the real erg movement despite higher sport visual realism.

### Sport-action fidelity

Sport-action fidelity refers to whether the visible action follows the logic of the sport: in rowing, catch, drive, finish/release, recovery, oarlock motion, blade movement, feathering/squaring, and boat response. Boat-centered XR rowing may increase sport-action fidelity even when device/body congruence is reduced.

## What not to overclaim

- Do not claim this paper proves VR rowing embodiment.
- Do not claim this paper is about rowing specifically.
- Do not claim the thesis evaluates sport performance transfer unless it does.
- Do not claim VR training generally works for all sports or all skills.
- Do not claim visual realism alone produces valid sport transfer.
- Do not let this source pull the thesis into motor-learning/training effectiveness as the main topic.
- Do not use it as an ownership/agency source.
- Do not use it as a questionnaire source.
