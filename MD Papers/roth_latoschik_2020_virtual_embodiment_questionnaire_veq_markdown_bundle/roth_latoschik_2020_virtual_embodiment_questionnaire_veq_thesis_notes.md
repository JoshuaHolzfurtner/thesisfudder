# Thesis notes — Roth & Latoschik 2020, “Construction of the Virtual Embodiment Questionnaire (VEQ)”

## Citation identity

Roth, D., & Latoschik, M. E. (2020). *Construction of the Virtual Embodiment Questionnaire (VEQ).* IEEE Transactions on Visualization and Computer Graphics, 26(12), 3546–3556. https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2020.3023603

*Virtual Embodiment Questionnaire (VEQ) - English*, distributed via virtualembodimentscale.com / virtualembodimentquestionnaire website.

## What this source bundle is

This bundle combines two documents:

1. The peer-reviewed VEQ construction paper by Roth & Latoschik.
2. The English questionnaire sheet containing the final item wording and scoring instructions.

This combination is useful because the peer-reviewed paper provides the validation and theoretical rationale, while the questionnaire sheet provides the practical item list and scoring format.

## What this paper is

Roth & Latoschik construct and validate the Virtual Embodiment Questionnaire (VEQ), a self-report scale for measuring perceptual aspects of embodiment in virtual reality. The paper responds to a measurement problem in VR embodiment research: previous studies often adapted individual Rubber Hand Illusion items or used custom item collections, making results hard to compare.

The authors review previous embodiment and virtual embodiment literature, develop questionnaire items, and test the factor structure using confirmatory factor analysis. Their analysis confirms three VEQ factors:

1. **Ownership** / Acceptance:
   - the perception that the virtual body is one’s own body.

2. **Agency** / Control:
   - the perception of controlling and causing the virtual body’s movements.

3. **Change**:
   - perceived change in one’s own body schema.

The scale construction uses data from three experiments with N = 196. A fourth validation study with N = 22 investigates effects of latency and latency jitter on the VEQ factors.

## Why it matters for the XR rowing thesis

This source is directly useful for the thesis method section because the thesis needs a concrete, citable way to measure virtual embodiment. The thesis compares two XR rowing modes:

1. Erg-centered / high-congruence mode:
   - real erg handle, seat, and body movement mapped closely to the avatar.

2. Boat-centered / sport-depiction mode:
   - real erg rowing is visually represented as on-water boat/oar rowing, with more sport-faithful depiction but less exact handle/oar congruence.

The VEQ maps especially well to the core dependent variables:

- **Ownership / Acceptance** can measure whether the virtual rowing body/body parts feel like the user’s own body.
- **Agency / Control** can measure whether the virtual rowing body movements feel controlled, caused by, and synchronized with the user’s own rowing movement.
- **Change** is probably less central unless the thesis investigates body-schema changes or altered avatar proportions.

For this thesis, the strongest use is to treat VEQ Acceptance/Ownership and VEQ Control/Agency as the primary self-report embodiment measures. VEQ Change can be included as optional or exploratory, but it is not the core construct unless the thesis explicitly studies body-schema change.

## Core findings / cheat sheet

- VEQ was created because previous VR embodiment studies lacked a consistent validated measurement instrument.
- The authors identify embodiment as related to body ownership, agency, and self-location in the broader literature.
- The final VEQ factors are ownership, agency, and perceived body-schema change.
- The final VEQ uses four items per factor.
- Ownership reliability in the CFA sample: Cronbach’s alpha approximately .783.
- Agency reliability in the CFA sample: Cronbach’s alpha approximately .764.
- Change reliability in the CFA sample: Cronbach’s alpha approximately .765.
- The paper includes a validation study manipulating latency and latency jitter.
- The English questionnaire sheet gives the practical 1–7 Likert format and scoring process.
- VEQ should be measured immediately after exposure / induction of virtual embodiment.
- VEQ does not directly preserve self-location as a final factor, even though self-location is discussed in the theoretical background.
- The companion sheet is appropriate for item wording, but the peer-reviewed paper should be cited for validation.

## Relation to thesis concepts

### Sense of embodiment

High relevance. VEQ is a practical measurement source for virtual embodiment. It does not replace Kilteni et al. as the main conceptual source, but it is highly useful for empirical measurement.

### Body ownership

Very high relevance. The VEQ Ownership / Acceptance factor is directly usable for the thesis’s ownership outcome.

Relevant VEQ items:
- It felt like the virtual body was my body.
- It felt like the virtual body parts were my body parts.
- The virtual body felt like a human body.
- It felt like the virtual body belonged to me.

For the XR rowing thesis, this can be adapted only minimally, for example by keeping the wording “virtual body” or specifying “virtual rowing body” if necessary. Avoid changing item wording too much if the scale is treated as validated.

### Agency

Very high relevance. The VEQ Agency / Control factor is directly usable for the thesis’s agency outcome.

Relevant VEQ items:
- The movements of the virtual body felt like they were my movements.
- I felt like I was controlling the movements of the virtual body.
- I felt like I was causing the movements of the virtual body.
- The movements of the virtual body were in sync with my own movements.

This is especially relevant for comparing the erg-centered and boat-centered modes, because the boat-centered mode may look more rowing-faithful but may feel less directly controlled if virtual oar/hand movement deviates from the real erg handle path.

### Self-location

Important caveat. Roth & Latoschik discuss self-location as a component of embodiment in the theoretical background, but self-location is not one of the final VEQ factors. Therefore VEQ alone is not enough if the thesis wants to empirically measure self-location as a separate subcomponent.

Options:
- Add one or more exploratory self-location items from existing embodiment/presence literature.
- Measure self-location through a separate validated instrument if available.
- Discuss self-location theoretically but make clear that the empirical VEQ focus is ownership and agency.
- Use presence/place items separately if the aim is more about being located in the virtual rowing environment rather than body-location in the strict embodiment sense.

### Temporal congruence / latency

Relevant. The validation study manipulates latency and latency jitter, making VEQ useful in a thesis where temporal congruence matters. Use Waltemate et al. as the stronger direct latency source, and Roth & Latoschik as the measurement-instrument source.

### Spatial alignment

Indirect relevance. VEQ does not measure spatial alignment directly. However, poor spatial alignment could reduce ownership and agency scores if the virtual body/handle no longer feels like it corresponds to the user’s real body and action.

Add custom exploratory alignment items if needed:
- The virtual handle felt spatially aligned with the real handle.
- The virtual body position felt aligned with my real body position.
- The virtual rowing setup felt correctly aligned with the real rowing machine.

These should be marked as custom/exploratory, not as validated VEQ items.

### Avatar realism / morphology

Moderate relevance. The paper discusses appearance, personalization, behavioral realism, and virtual body representation. However, if the thesis does not compare different avatar appearances, this should remain background/control material.

### Sport-action realism

VEQ does not measure sport realism. Add custom exploratory items for the thesis-specific comparison:
- The rowing movement looked like real rowing.
- The virtual rowing action felt believable as rowing.
- The oars, boat, and stroke motion felt rowing-faithful.
- The virtual rowing motion matched what I expected rowing to look like.

These should not be mixed into VEQ scoring.

## How it maps to my thesis modes

### Erg-centered mode

VEQ Agency is likely highly relevant here. The erg-centered mode should score strongly if users feel:

- the virtual body movements are their movements,
- they control the virtual body,
- they cause the virtual body movement,
- virtual movement is synchronized with their real movement.

VEQ Ownership may also be higher if the avatar/handle/body alignment makes the virtual body feel like the user’s own.

### Boat-centered mode

The boat-centered mode might produce different outcomes:

- It may score higher on custom sport-realism or rowing-plausibility items.
- It may score lower on VEQ Agency if the visible oar/hand movement feels less directly controlled by the real erg handle.
- It may still score well on VEQ Ownership if the avatar body is convincing and responsive enough.
- The interesting thesis question is whether sport-action plausibility can compensate for reduced device/handle congruence.

### Core tradeoff

VEQ gives the thesis a way to separate:

- **embodiment measures**: ownership and agency,
- **sport-realism measures**: custom/exploratory rowing realism items,
- **preference measures**: user preference and qualitative comments.

This is important because a participant may prefer the boat-centered mode or rate it as more realistic rowing while still giving lower ownership or agency ratings.

## What not to overclaim

- Do not call VEQ an “industry standard” unless you can show adoption evidence. Better: “validated self-report questionnaire.”
- Do not claim VEQ fully measures all aspects of sense of embodiment as defined by Kilteni et al.; VEQ’s final factors are ownership, agency, and change.
- Do not claim VEQ directly measures self-location.
- Do not treat custom rowing-realism or alignment items as validated VEQ subscales.
- Do not modify VEQ item wording heavily and still present it as the unchanged validated instrument.
- Do not use VEQ Change as a central outcome unless body-schema change is actually relevant to the study.
- Do not claim questionnaire scores prove objective embodiment; they are subjective self-report measures.
- Do not claim VEQ alone can distinguish all aspects of presence, plausibility, sport realism, and embodiment.

## Recommended thesis method wording

The study uses the Virtual Embodiment Questionnaire (VEQ) by Roth and Latoschik as a validated self-report instrument for assessing virtual embodiment. The main analysis focuses on the VEQ Ownership/Acceptance and Agency/Control subscales, because these map directly to the thesis question of whether users experience the virtual rowing body and movement as their own and under their control. The VEQ Change subscale is treated as optional or exploratory, since the study does not primarily manipulate avatar body shape or body schema. Additional thesis-specific items assess rowing realism, handle/body alignment, and preference; these items are treated as exploratory and are not included in VEQ scoring.

## Candidate short thesis sentence

The Virtual Embodiment Questionnaire provides validated ownership and agency subscales that can be used to compare whether erg-centered or boat-centered XR rowing produces a stronger subjective sense that the virtual rowing body and movement are one’s own.

## Candidate appendix note

The VEQ items were administered on a 1–7 Likert scale immediately after each embodiment condition. Ownership/Acceptance was calculated as the mean of the four ownership items, and Agency/Control as the mean of the four agency items. Custom rowing-specific items were analyzed separately and not included in VEQ scores.
