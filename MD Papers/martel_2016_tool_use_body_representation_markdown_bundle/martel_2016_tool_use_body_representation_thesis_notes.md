## Why this paper is useful for the thesis

This review is useful as a conceptual bridge between embodiment, peripersonal space, body schema, body image, and tool incorporation. For a VR rowing thesis, it is especially relevant because rowing on an erg while seeing virtual oars/boat bodies creates a tool-like extension problem: the user acts through an interface that may alter action space, body representation for action, and perceived bodily boundaries.

## Core takeaways

1. **Tool-use can remap peripersonal space (PPS).** The paper reviews monkey, patient, and healthy-participant evidence that tool-use can extend PPS, especially when the tool functionally extends reach.

2. **Tool-use may also modify body representations, not only surrounding space.** The authors emphasize that tool-use can affect the body schema - the action-oriented representation of body size/posture used to control movement.

3. **Body schema and body image should not be collapsed.** The paper distinguishes body schema, body image, and body structural description. This is useful when separating unconscious motor/action representations from conscious visual or semantic representations of the body.

4. **Active, functional use matters.** A recurring theme is that tool incorporation is strongest when the tool is used functionally, not merely held. However, observation, imagery, and multisensory synchrony may also contribute under some circumstances.

5. **Morphology and function of the tool matter.** A tool that lengthens the arm affects arm/transport representations; a hand-shaped or hand-widening tool may affect hand/grasp-related representations.

6. **The paper cautions against treating tool-use as a universal explanation.** The authors frame tool-use as useful but not universal; effects vary by representation, task, input/output modality, and the type of tool-use paradigm.

## Thesis-relevant mapping to VR rowing

- **Erg handle / oar interface:** A rowing handle or virtual oar may be discussed as an action interface/tool rather than simply as an avatar limb.
- **Boat-centric mode:** Seeing the avatar hold oars may create a sport-realistic tool-use representation that differs from the real handle trajectory.
- **Erg-centric mode:** Precise mapping of real handle and seat movement may better preserve visuomotor congruence and agency, while reducing the tool-extension mismatch.
- **Embodiment framing:** This paper can support the claim that action tools can reshape body-space/body-schema representations, but it should not be used alone to claim body ownership over the virtual oar or boat.

## Useful concepts to track in your helper MD

- Peripersonal space (PPS): space immediately surrounding the body; multisensory action/protection interface.
- Body schema (BS): action-oriented, mostly unconscious, sensorimotor representation of body posture, shape, size, and position.
- Body image (BI): more conscious, visual/semantic/affective representation of the body.
- Body structural description (BSD): visuospatial map of body parts and their topological relationships.
- Tool incorporation / tool embodiment: potentially different effects depending on whether one means PPS extension, body schema change, ownership, or prosthesis embodiment.

## Do not overclaim

- Do not cite this paper as proof that every tool becomes part of the body.
- Do not equate peripersonal-space extension with full sense of embodiment.
- Do not equate body schema plasticity with body ownership.
- Do not assume tool-use effects are permanent; the review repeatedly discusses transient effects and context dependence.
- Do not directly transfer arm-reaching studies to rowing without qualifying the difference in task, tool, and movement ecology.
