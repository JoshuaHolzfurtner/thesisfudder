# Thesis Notes - Mottelson et al. (2023) Body Ownership Illusions in VR

## Source

Mottelson, A., Muresan, A., Hornbaek, K., & Makransky, G. (2023). *A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of the Effectiveness of Body Ownership Illusions in Virtual Reality*. ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction, 30(5), Article 76. DOI: 10.1145/3590767.

## What this paper is

This is a systematic review and meta-analysis of body ownership illusion (BOI) studies in virtual reality. It reviews 111 articles published from 2010 to 2021 and reports data from 4,925 participants. The paper focuses on HMD-based VR studies in which participants experience a virtual avatar as a replacement for their actual body.

## Why it matters for a VR rowing / embodiment thesis

This paper is best used as a high-level empirical support and methods paper, not as the primary conceptual definition of sense of embodiment. For conceptual framing, Kilteni, Groten, and Slater remain more direct. Mottelson et al. are useful because they aggregate a large body of VR BOI work and quantify which manipulations tend to affect body ownership and agency.

For a rowing setup comparing an erg-congruent avatar mode with a boat/oar-based avatar mode, the most relevant point is that visuomotor synchrony/asynchrony is reported as the strongest manipulation for body ownership and agency. This supports treating the real-to-virtual mapping of handle, hands, seat, and avatar movement as a central experimental variable rather than a merely technical implementation detail.

## Key takeaways

### 1. Visuomotor congruence is highly important

The review reports that visuo-motor asynchrony has the largest effect on subjective body ownership among the examined avatar manipulations, and also the largest effect on agency. This is directly relevant to any VR rowing comparison where one condition prioritizes precise bodily movement correspondence and another prioritizes a more realistic sport depiction with a less exact movement match.

### 2. Agency and body ownership should be separated

Across the reviewed studies, agency tends to be reported higher than body ownership. This matters because a participant can feel strong control over a virtual avatar without necessarily feeling that the virtual body is their own body. In a rowing thesis, this supports reporting agency and ownership separately instead of collapsing them into one undifferentiated embodiment score.

### 3. Body ownership effects are often modest

The paper emphasizes that embodiment of virtual avatars generally leads to modest body ownership and slightly higher agency. This is useful as a reality check: even well-designed VR body illusions may not produce overwhelmingly strong ownership scores.

### 4. The field has measurement problems

The authors argue that BOI research lacks standardization across tasks, questionnaires, measurement instruments, and analysis approaches. Many studies use adapted or ad hoc questionnaires, often tracing back to Rubber Hand Illusion items rather than VR-specific validated instruments. For thesis writing, this is useful justification for careful construct labeling and conservative interpretation.

### 5. Terminology is messy

The paper explicitly discusses terminological confusion: body ownership, embodiment, self-presence, agency, and related constructs are sometimes used inconsistently. This supports being precise in your thesis, e.g. distinguishing sense of agency, body ownership, self-location, and broader sense of embodiment.

### 6. Mirrors are not a magic fix

The review reports that the presence of virtual mirrors did not clearly improve body ownership and may have limited or even negative effects compared to studies without mirrors. This is useful if considering mirrors in a VR rowing environment; mirrors may help users inspect avatars, but they should not be assumed to automatically strengthen embodiment.

## Thesis-use suggestions

Use this paper to support claims such as:

- VR body ownership illusion research has accumulated enough empirical work for meta-analytic comparison.
- Visuomotor synchrony is one of the strongest known manipulations of virtual body ownership and agency.
- Body ownership and agency are related but separable components and should be measured/report separately.
- BOI research has substantial methodological heterogeneity, so results should be interpreted conservatively.
- Questionnaire measures in VR embodiment research are often adapted from earlier body illusion paradigms and are not always formally validated for VR.

Avoid overclaiming:

- Do not cite this paper as saying that visuomotor congruence is the only factor that matters. It also discusses perspective, abstraction, appearance, and visuotactile congruence.
- Do not use it as the sole definition of embodiment. It is more of an evidence synthesis and methodology review.
- Do not assume meta-analytic averages apply directly to rowing; rowing includes sport-specific tools, rhythm, effort, water/boat representation, and ergometer constraints.
- Do not treat subjective ownership scores as direct proof of full embodiment.

## Relevance to erg-centered vs boat-centered VR rowing

The erg-centered condition likely aligns with the paper's strongest evidence base because it can maximize visuomotor congruence: the user's real handle, hands, seat, and avatar motion can correspond closely.

The boat-centered/oar-based condition may trade some visuomotor congruence for sport-realistic depiction. This paper helps frame the central empirical question: whether higher visuomotor congruence produces stronger embodiment than a more realistic rowing depiction, or whether realism, task identity, and sport context compensate for lower movement correspondence.

## Recommended reading order

Read carefully:

1. Summary / Abstract
2. Objectives
3. Effects of Avatar Manipulation
4. Subjective Measurement of Embodiment in VR
5. Terminological Confusion
6. Discussion and recommendations

Skim:

- The large study tables unless you need to trace a specific included paper.
- Appendices unless you need questionnaire details, statistical practice, or robustness checks.

## Extraction caveats

The prose extracts well. Large tables and figures are less trustworthy as Markdown. For exact values from forest plots, tables, or questionnaire summaries, verify against the PDF.
