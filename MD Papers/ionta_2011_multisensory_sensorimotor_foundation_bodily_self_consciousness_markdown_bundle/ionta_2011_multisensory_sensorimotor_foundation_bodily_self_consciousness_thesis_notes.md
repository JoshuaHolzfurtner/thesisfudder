# Thesis notes — Ionta, Gassert & Blanke (2011), “Multi-sensory and sensorimotor foundation of bodily self-consciousness – an interdisciplinary approach”

## Citation identity

Ionta, S., Gassert, R., & Blanke, O. (2011). Multi-sensory and sensorimotor foundation of bodily self-consciousness – an interdisciplinary approach. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2, 383.

## What this paper is

This is a short review article on bodily self-consciousness from an interdisciplinary perspective combining:
- cognitive neuroscience,
- virtual reality,
- robotics,
- multisensory integration,
- clinical evidence from out-of-body experiences,
- full-body illusion experiments.

The main claim is that bodily self-consciousness is not only a high-level cognitive phenomenon. It is strongly grounded in low-level multisensory and sensorimotor integration of bodily signals.

The paper focuses especially on:
- self-location,
- first-person perspective,
- self-identification,
- multisensory conflict,
- temporo-parietal junction (TPJ),
- virtual reality and robotic methods for controlled embodiment experiments.

## Why it matters for the XR rowing thesis

This is a good core-background source for your embodiment framework, especially for self-location and full-body bodily self-consciousness.

Your thesis does not only involve whether users feel that virtual hands are theirs. It also asks whether the user feels located in and acting through a rowing body in an XR scene.

Ionta et al. helps support the claim that:
- bodily self-consciousness is built from multisensory and sensorimotor signals,
- virtual reality can experimentally manipulate those signals,
- self-location and identification can shift through visual-tactile and visual-body conflicts,
- robotics and VR are suitable methods for precise experimental control of bodily self-consciousness.

## Core concepts / cheat sheet

### 1) Bodily self-consciousness is multisensory

The paper argues that the bodily self depends on integration of many bodily sensory inputs:
- visual,
- auditory,
- vestibular,
- somatosensory,
- motor,
- visceral.

Thesis use:
Your rowing system combines visual avatar/body feedback, proprioception from real rowing movement, vestibular/spatial orientation, motor effort, tactile handle/seat feedback, and potentially audio/water cues. This makes it a bodily self-consciousness problem, not just a graphics or input-mapping problem.

### 2) Local body illusions vs full-body illusions

The paper distinguishes local body-part illusions from more global bodily self-consciousness manipulations.

Local examples:
- rubber hand illusion,
- numbness illusion,
- enfacement illusion.

Global/full-body examples:
- self-location changes,
- first-person perspective changes,
- self-identification with a virtual body.

Thesis use:
Your rowing app is closer to a full-body/avatar/action problem than a simple rubber-hand-only problem, even though hand/arm ownership remains important.

### 3) Three important components: self-location, first-person perspective, self-identification

The paper reviews three aspects of bodily self-consciousness:
- self-location: where the self is experienced as being located,
- first-person perspective: the viewpoint from which the world/body is experienced,
- self-identification: identification with a body.

This overlaps strongly with your SoE vocabulary:
- self-location maps directly to SoL,
- first-person perspective supports the VR viewpoint / perspective framing,
- self-identification is related to, but not identical with, ownership/identification.

Be careful:
The paper’s “self-identification” is not exactly the same as avatar identification in game studies. It is more about identifying with the seen body in full-body illusions.

### 4) Out-of-body experiences show abnormal self-location and perspective

The paper uses neurological out-of-body experiences as evidence that self-location and first-person perspective can be dissociated from the physical body.

Thesis use:
You probably do not need the clinical detail heavily. The useful point is that self-location and first-person perspective are real constructs with neurological grounding, not just vague VR feelings.

### 5) TPJ as a multisensory integration hub

The paper argues that the temporo-parietal junction (TPJ) is a key area for multisensory integration of bodily self-consciousness.

It reviews evidence that:
- TPJ dysfunction is linked to out-of-body experiences,
- electrical stimulation of TPJ can induce OBE-like phenomena,
- TPJ activity reflects experimentally induced changes in self-location and first-person perspective.

Thesis use:
Mention lightly if you want neural grounding. Do not overdo neuroscience unless your thesis needs it.

### 6) Full-body illusions depend on multisensory conflict and synchrony

The paper reviews studies where participants see their body from a displaced or third-person perspective and receive synchronous or asynchronous stroking.

Synchronous visuo-tactile stimulation can increase:
- self-identification with the virtual body,
- illusory touch,
- shifts in self-location toward the virtual body,
- physiological response to threat.

Thesis use:
This supports your claim that synchrony/congruence can manipulate bodily self-consciousness and self-location, not just local ownership of hands.

### 7) Robotics and VR enable precise embodiment experiments

The paper emphasizes that robotic systems and VR can standardize stimulation and control sensory conflicts better than manual experimenter-driven setups.

Thesis use:
This is useful because your prototype similarly uses engineered spatial alignment, tracking, avatar mapping, and controlled visual/body correspondence as experimental variables.

## Relation to your thesis modes

### Erg-centered mode

The erg-centered mode gives strong multisensory and sensorimotor agreement:
- real seat motion,
- real handle motion,
- real effort,
- visual avatar/handle motion,
- first-person VR perspective,
- possibly self-location within the avatar/erg.

This mode can be framed as maximizing bottom-up multisensory congruence for bodily self-consciousness.

### Boat-centered mode

The boat-centered mode changes the mapping:
- the user physically rows on an erg,
- but sees a more sport-realistic rowing body/oar/boat depiction.

This can be discussed as a controlled conflict/transformation between bodily sensorimotor evidence and visual sport depiction. Ionta et al. supports the idea that such conflicts can influence self-location, self-identification, and embodied experience.

## Relation to other papers in the thesis stack

### Kilteni et al.
Kilteni gives the structured SoE components:

- agency,
- ownership.

Ionta et al. gives a complementary bodily self-consciousness / neuroscience angle:
- self-location,
- first-person perspective,
- self-identification,
- TPJ,
- multisensory integration.

### Skarbez et al.
Skarbez helps separate presence, immersion, fidelity, coherence, and embodiment.
Ionta helps ground embodiment/self-location in multisensory body processes.

### Kalckert & Ehrsson
Kalckert separates agency and ownership in moving rubber hand paradigms.
Ionta broadens the view to full-body self-location and perspective.

### Waltemate
Waltemate gives latency/synchrony evidence in full-body avatar feedback.
Ionta gives a broader foundation for why multisensory congruence matters for bodily self-consciousness.

## Candidate thesis paragraph

“Bodily self-consciousness can be understood as grounded in multisensory and sensorimotor integration rather than as a purely high-level cognitive judgment. Ionta et al. review evidence that self-location, first-person perspective, and self-identification can be experimentally manipulated through multisensory conflicts in virtual-reality and robotic setups, and that temporo-parietal regions contribute to integrating bodily signals. This is directly relevant for XR rowing because the user’s virtual rowing body is experienced through a combination of visual avatar feedback, real handle and seat motion, proprioceptive and motor signals, and the first-person perspective of the VR scene.”

## Candidate short thesis sentence

“XR rowing can therefore be treated as a bodily self-consciousness problem: the avatar and rowing action are experienced through the integration of visual, proprioceptive, tactile, motor, and spatial cues.”

## What not to overclaim

Do not treat this paper as a detailed SoE taxonomy source replacing Kilteni et al.

Do not claim your thesis investigates neural TPJ mechanisms unless you actually measure them.

Do not imply that your rowing setup induces out-of-body experiences. OBE evidence is used here only to show dissociability and neural grounding of self-location/perspective.

Do not collapse self-identification in this paper with game-style avatar identification. They overlap conceptually but come from different traditions.

Do not overuse neuroscience in the main thesis if your contribution is system + embodiment evaluation.
