# Concept Source Map

This file maps key concepts and claims in the thesis to supporting sources. It is a navigational aid for both human and AI collaborators. It is **not** final thesis prose; rather, it helps determine which sources back up which ideas, suggests safe wording, and flags potential pitfalls.

For each concept, list the primary sources (summaries or notes), describe the core idea, propose safe ways to phrase it in the thesis, and note anything to avoid. Adjust or expand these entries as your literature review grows.

## Template for a Concept

### Concept Name

- **Sources:** (list files such as `kilteni2012soe.md`, `slater2009presence.md`)  
- **Core idea:** Briefly describe the essence of the concept.  
- **Safe thesis wording:** Suggest cautious phrasing that aligns with the literature.  
- **Avoid:** List wording or interpretations that would overstate or misrepresent the source.

---

## Example: Sense of Embodiment

- **Sources:** `kilteni2012soe.md`, `slater2009presence.md`  
- **Core idea:** The sense of embodiment refers to the extent to which a body is experienced as one’s own. It is commonly broken down into self‑location, agency, and body ownership.  
- **Safe thesis wording:** “The sense of embodiment is often conceptualized as comprising three components: self‑location, agency, and body ownership.”  
- **Avoid:** “Embodiment is always and only these three components” or suggesting that the definitions are universally agreed upon without caveats.

---

## Example: Rubber Hand Illusion

- **Sources:** `botvinick1998rhi.md`  
- **Core idea:** Synchronous visuotactile stimulation can induce ownership over a visible artificial hand. Participants may misattribute the rubber hand as their own when visual and tactile cues match.  
- **Safe thesis wording:** “In the rubber hand illusion, participants can experience a sense of ownership over a fake hand when seen and felt touch are temporally aligned.”  
- **Avoid:** Implying that the rubber hand illusion demonstrates full‑body embodiment or directly applies to rowing without further justification.
