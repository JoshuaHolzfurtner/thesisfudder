# Chapter 1 — Introduction — Back of Mind

## Purpose

Introduce VR indoor rowing as a case where realistic sport depiction and visuomotor congruence can conflict. Motivate the need for a new system that can compare these approaches. Present the core problem: should a VR rowing system prioritize precise alignment with the real ergometer and the user’s body, or realistic boat/oar depiction? State the research questions around **sense of ownership (SoO)**, **sense of agency (SoA)**, and **sense of embodiment (SoE)**.

## Reader state before this chapter

The reader may be generally familiar with VR and rowing but may not know why indoor rowing is an interesting case for embodiment research or why rowing visuals and congruence might conflict.

## Reader state after this chapter

The reader should understand: (1) why VR indoor rowing is challenging and interesting; (2) what the two competing design goals are (visuomotor congruence versus sport realism); (3) the overarching research questions; and (4) a high‑level overview of the thesis structure.

## Core claims

- The problem of whether to prioritize congruence or realism in VR rowing has not been systematically studied.
- Embodiment subcomponents (SoO, SoA, SoL) can be influenced by design choices in XR rowing systems.
- The thesis compares two representation modes to investigate how embodiment is shaped by these choices.

## Allowed claims

- Introduce the system as a testbed for embodiment research.
- Frame the thesis as exploring design trade‑offs rather than proving one mode superior.

## Forbidden overclaims

- Do not imply that the results are known in advance or that one mode is obviously superior.
- Avoid statements that generalize beyond the scope of indoor rowing or XR sports.
