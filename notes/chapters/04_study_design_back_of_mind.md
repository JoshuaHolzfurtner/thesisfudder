# Chapter 4 — Study Design and Method — Back of Mind

## Purpose

Describe how the user study is designed to compare the ergometer‑based and boat‑based modes. Outline the experimental conditions, participants, procedure, measures, and analysis plan. Ensure that the design can answer the research questions about how visuomotor congruence and sport realism influence embodiment.

## Reader state before this chapter

The reader understands the system and its two modes but does not know how the comparison will be conducted or what data will be collected.

## Reader state after this chapter

The reader should know exactly how the study is structured: number of conditions, how participants experience them, what questionnaires and metrics are used, and how the data will be analyzed. This provides the necessary context for interpreting the results.

## Core claims

- The study design (e.g., within‑subject or between‑subject) is appropriate for comparing the two modes.
- The chosen measures (SoO, SoA, SoE, presence, realism, optional physiology) are sufficient to capture embodiment differences.
- Statistical analyses are planned a priori (e.g., ANOVA, paired t‑tests).

## Allowed claims

- Explain the rationale for sample size and participant demographics.
- Justify the choice of measures and the procedure.

## Forbidden overclaims

- Do not state that the study design will definitively prove a causal relationship beyond its scope.
- Avoid implying that the study will generalize beyond similar XR sports contexts.

## Required sources

- Literature on embodiment measures (questionnaires for SoO, SoA, etc.).
- Methodological references for VR user studies (e.g., guidelines on sample sizes, counterbalancing).

## Required project details

- Number of participants, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and recruitment process.
- Experimental conditions (order of modes, counterbalancing strategy).
- Description of questionnaires and any physiological measures (e.g., details about heart‑rate monitor, threat stimuli).
- Statistical analysis plan.

## Open TODOs

- Decide on specific questionnaire scales and their citations.
- Determine sample size based on power analysis or precedent.
- Develop the procedure script (e.g., introduction, instructions, trial order).

## Tone

Precise and methodical. Convey rigor and transparency. Use future tense when describing planned procedures; switch to past tense after the study is complete.
