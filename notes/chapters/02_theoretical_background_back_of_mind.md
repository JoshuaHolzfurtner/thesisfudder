# Chapter 2 — Theoretical Background and Related Work — Back of Mind

## Purpose

Provide the conceptual and empirical foundations for the thesis. Introduce the key concepts of **sense of ownership (SoO)**, **sense of agency (SoA)**, **sense of self‑location (SoL)**, and **sense of embodiment (SoE)**. Discuss classic paradigms such as the **rubber hand illusion (RHI)** and how they inform VR embodiment research. Review literature on visuomotor congruence, multisensory integration, avatar realism, and VR sports applications. Optionally cover physiological threat measures as implicit indices of embodiment.

## Reader state before this chapter

The reader knows the research questions and has a basic motivation for the study but may not be familiar with embodiment theory or prior VR rowing work.

## Reader state after this chapter

The reader should have a clear understanding of: (1) the theoretical constructs being measured; (2) how embodiment has been studied (e.g., RHI); (3) why congruence and realism matter; and (4) existing VR sports and rowing systems.

## Core claims

- Embodiment can be decomposed into subcomponents (SoO, SoA, SoL).
- Visuomotor and multisensory congruence are key determinants of embodiment.
- Avatar realism and top‑down expectations also influence embodiment.
- Past VR sports projects often prioritize either realism or congruence but rarely study their trade‑off.
