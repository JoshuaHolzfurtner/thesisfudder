# Chapter 5 — Results — Back of Mind

## Purpose

Present the findings of the user study. Report quantitative and qualitative outcomes for each measure (e.g., SoO, SoA, SoE, presence, realism, physiological responses). Highlight differences between the ergometer‑based and boat‑based modes and report statistical significance where applicable.

## Reader state before this chapter

The reader knows the study design but has not seen the data. They expect to learn what was observed and how it relates to the hypotheses.

## Reader state after this chapter

The reader should understand the key numerical results, effect sizes, and any notable patterns or participant feedback. They should be able to follow how the data support or challenge the claims laid out earlier.

## Core claims

- Differences (or similarities) between modes are reported as observed, not predetermined.
- Results are presented transparently, including non‑significant findings.

## Allowed claims

- Use descriptive statistics, figures, and tables to convey patterns.
- Summarize participant comments where relevant (e.g., anecdotal insights on realism or comfort).

## Forbidden overclaims

- Do not interpret results here; leave interpretation to the Discussion chapter.
- Avoid implying causality beyond the experimental comparisons.

## Required sources

- No external sources are necessary here, but cite methods for statistical tests if helpful.

## Required project details

- Data for each measure (means, standard deviations, significance tests).
- Visualizations (e.g., bar charts, box plots) can be added later under ``figures/``.

- Determine how to anonymize participant comments (if included).

## Tone

Objective and factual. Use past tense. Provide sufficient detail for readers to evaluate the robustness of the findings.
