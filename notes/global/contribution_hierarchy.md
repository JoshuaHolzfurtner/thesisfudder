# Contribution Hierarchy

This document outlines the hierarchy of contributions made by the thesis. It helps to maintain clarity about what the thesis aims to deliver and prevents scope creep.

## 1. Developed XR rowing mechanism

Design and implementation of a virtual reality rowing mechanism that supports two representation modes: an ergometer‑congruent mode and a boat‑realistic mode. This includes the hardware integration (e.g., handle and seat tracking) and the software layer that maps physical inputs to avatar movements.

## 2. Implemented prototype with two modes

Construction of a working prototype, including the avatar, inverse kinematics, and visual environment. The prototype is used as the experimental apparatus to compare the two modes.

## 3. Conducted user evaluation

Design and execution of a study that investigates how the two modes affect sense of ownership, sense of agency, and other embodiment‑related measures. Data collection may include subjective questionnaires and optional physiological responses.

## 4. Interpreted findings and derived design implications

Analysis of the study results in the context of embodiment theory, multisensory congruence, and VR sports design. The discussion highlights trade‑offs between precise movement correspondence and sport realism and proposes guidelines for future XR sport and training systems.

## 5. Academic positioning

Situating the work within existing literature on embodiment, virtual reality sports, and human–machine interaction. The thesis does not aim to provide a new theoretical framework but rather applies and tests existing concepts in a novel domain.
