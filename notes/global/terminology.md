# Terminology

This glossary summarizes key concepts and terms used in the thesis. It is drawn from embodiment research and adapted to the context of XR rowing.

## Embodiment / Bodily self‑consciousness

The fundamental experience of being a self located in, and tied to, a body. In embodiment research, this experience is considered to emerge from multisensory and sensorimotor signals rather than purely abstract thought.

## Visuomotor / Multisensory congruence

The degree to which what one sees matches what one feels and does. Visuomotor congruence refers specifically to the alignment between visual and motoric signals (e.g., how well the movements we see match the movements we perform). Multisensory congruence extends this to include tactile, proprioceptive, and vestibular cues. Higher congruence generally strengthens embodiment, whereas conflicts can shift self‑identification and perceived location.

## Sense of Self‑Location (SoL)

The felt volume in space where one experiences oneself to be located. Under normal conditions, self‑location coincides with the physical body. In embodiment frameworks, self‑location is one of three core subcomponents of the sense of embodiment.

## Sense of Ownership (SoO) / Sense of Body Ownership (SoBO)

The feeling that a body part, or an entire body, is one’s own. Classic demonstrations like the rubber hand illusion show that synchronous seen and felt touch can induce ownership over a fake hand. Similar effects apply to virtual hands and bodies.

## Sense of Agency (SoA)

The feeling that one is the initiator or controller of an action or movement. In this thesis, SoA concerns whether the user perceives the stroke, handle motion, or avatar movement as being caused by their own actions. Although distinct from ownership, agency is often discussed alongside it.

## Sense of Embodiment (SoE)

The extent to which a (virtual) body is experienced as one’s own embodied self. A common framework treats SoE as comprising three subcomponents: self‑location, agency, and body ownership. One experiences SoE toward a body *B* if one experiences at least one of these senses at a minimal level; full SoE corresponds to experiencing all three strongly at once.

## Bottom‑up influences

Sensory‑driven processes arising from incoming signals from the body and environment. In embodiment research, these include correlations between what one feels and what one sees, such as synchronized visuotactile or visuoproprioceptive stimulation. In this thesis, examples include the user’s real handle motion, seat motion, proprioception, muscle effort, and the temporal alignment between these cues and the avatar’s movements.

## Top‑down influences

Cognitive and perceptual expectations that shape how sensory information is interpreted. Examples include whether the virtual body appears human‑like, anatomically plausible, self‑similar, or recognizable as one’s own. In this thesis, top‑down influences include the realism of the avatar’s hands, the similarity to the participant’s body, and whether the displayed rowing body looks plausible as the participant’s own body in action.

## Rubber Hand Illusion (RHI)

A classic paradigm for studying body ownership. In the RHI, a participant’s real hand is hidden while a rubber hand is stroked synchronously with the participant’s real hand. Under synchronous stimulation, the rubber hand may be experienced as one’s own. The RHI illustrates how visual, tactile, and proprioceptive information contribute to body representation and ownership. Threat‐based measures (e.g., threatening the rubber hand with harm) are sometimes used as implicit indices of embodiment.

## Cyborg’s Dilemma

The paradox that interfaces become more powerful and natural‐feeling as they are incorporated into the user’s body schema, but that such incorporation requires the user to adapt to the logic of the machine. In this thesis, the cyborg’s dilemma frames the trade‑off between two rowing representations: an ergometer‑centered mode that maximizes congruence and bodily incorporation, and a boat‑centered mode that preserves sport realism at the cost of precise alignment.
