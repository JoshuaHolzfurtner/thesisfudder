# Claims Register

This register lists the core claims and hypotheses that guide the thesis. Each claim includes its status, permissible wording, forbidden wording, and supporting evidence or gaps. The register helps ensure that AI assistance remains aligned with the author’s intentions and avoids overclaiming.

## C001 — Ergometer‑congruent mode is designed to support stronger embodiment

- **Status:** Hypothesis / design rationale
- **Allowed wording:** "designed to", "aims to", "may support"
- **Forbidden wording:** "proves", "guarantees", "objectively increases"
- **Evidence:** Implementation: real handle and seat motions are mapped with high spatial and temporal precision onto the avatar. Literature suggests that visuomotor congruence enhances embodiment. Empirical evidence will come from the user study.

## C002 — Boat‑realistic mode increases sport realism but reduces visuomotor congruence

- **Status:** Hypothesis / design rationale
- **Allowed wording:** "prioritizes sport realism", "may trade off visuomotor congruence", "offers a more realistic rowing depiction at the cost of alignment"
- **Forbidden wording:** "proves that realism reduces embodiment", "always reduces embodiment"
- **Evidence:** In the boat‑realistic mode, the virtual oar motion is based on realistic rowing animations rather than the exact trajectory of the erg handle. The mapping intentionally sacrifices spatial congruence to depict an oar that slices through virtual water. The study will test how users perceive ownership and agency under this mode.

## C003 — Embodiment depends on spatial and temporal congruence and avatar realism

- **Status:** Literature claim
- **Allowed wording:** "is influenced by", "depends on", "is shaped by"
- **Forbidden wording:** "is solely determined by", "is always increased by"
- **Evidence:** Research on embodiment and body ownership (e.g., the rubber hand illusion) shows that synchronized multisensory signals and realistic appearance contribute to body ownership, agency, and self‑location. Avatar realism can modulate the strength of these effects.

## C004 — Sense of embodiment comprises self‑location, agency, and body ownership

- **Status:** Literature claim
- **Allowed wording:** "is often conceptualized as comprising", "includes the subcomponents of"
- **Forbidden wording:** "is limited to", "excludes other components"
- **Evidence:** The embodiment literature defines the sense of embodiment (SoE) as the extent to which a body is experienced as one's own, typically broken down into self‑location (SoL), sense of agency (SoA), and sense of ownership (SoO). This framework is used throughout the thesis.

## C005 — Design trade‑offs illustrate a version of the cyborg’s dilemma

- **Status:** Interpretive claim
- **Allowed wording:** "illustrates", "can be interpreted as", "may reflect"
- **Forbidden wording:** "proves that", "demonstrates that"
- **Evidence:** The prototype presents a choice between close bodily incorporation of the ergometer (maximizing congruence) and a sport‑faithful depiction that diverges from the user's real motion. This echoes the cyborg’s dilemma, in which interfaces become more natural as they are incorporated but require users to adapt to the machine’s logic.
