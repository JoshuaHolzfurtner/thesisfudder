## Chapter-Level Argument Summary

Chapter 1 currently argues that indoor rowing is a useful XR embodiment case because a rowing ergometer gives the user real forceful rowing-like effort, but removes the boat, oars, water, and spatial movement that structure on-water rowing. VR can restore some visual and contextual features, but it cannot remove the physical fact that the user is still on an ergometer. The chapter therefore frames the thesis around a trade-off between an ergometer-congruent representation, which preserves the real action loop, and a boat-centric or boat-realistic representation, which preserves the recognizable sport depiction. The empirical question is deliberately comparative and exploratory: how do these mappings affect agency, ownership, self-location, overall embodiment, perceived rowing realism, comfort, and preference?

The strongest Introduction source spine is:

- Rowing/ergometer difference: `lamb1989kinematicComparison`, `soperHume2004rowingTechnique`, `rauterEtAl2013rowingTransfer`
- VR sport and VR rowing context: `neumannEtAl2018interactiveVrSport`, `murrayEtAl2016vrRowingPresence`, `arndtEtAl2018vrRowingWorkouts`
- Embodiment framework: `kilteniEtAl2012senseEmbodiment`
- Synchrony, ownership, and agency: `botvinickCohen1998rubberHand`, `kalckertEhrsson2012movingRubberHand`, optionally `waltemateEtAl2016latency`
- Visual motion and self-motion wording: `niehorster2021opticFlowHistory`, optionally `kooijmanEtAl2024measuringVection` or `palmisanoEtAl2015vectionChallenges`
- Plausibility/presence distinction: `slater2009placePlausibility`
- VR action caution: `harrisEtAl2019visionForAction`
