\section{Rowing-Specific and Experience Measures}
\label{sec:study_design_measures_rowing_specific}

The established embodiment items are complemented by custom rowing-specific items. These items are necessary because the thesis does not compare embodiment in an abstract avatar task. It compares two ways of representing the same real ergometer action: one that preserves direct correspondence with the physical machine, and one that restores a more recognizable boat-and-oar rowing depiction. Rowing biomechanics and simulator work make this distinction meaningful rather than cosmetic: a central ergometer handle can support controlled indoor rowing, but it does not reproduce all on-water oar handling, blade, and boat mechanics \parencite{lamb1989kinematicComparison,soperHume2004rowingTechnique,rauterEtAl2013rowingTransfer}.

The custom per-condition items therefore target thesis-specific dimensions that the VEQ does not measure directly:

\begin{itemize}
    \item embodied action congruence between the participant's real ergometer movement and the virtual rowing body,
    \item the real handle as a physical, haptic, and proprioceptive anchor for the seen virtual hands, handle, or oars,
    \item acceptability of the virtual hand/oar mapping, especially where the boat-realistic mode retargets the real handle relation into an oar-like action,
    \item sport-action plausibility of the rowing depiction, including whether the shown body, oars, and stroke phases read as rowing on water rather than generic seated movement,
    \item perceived conflict between the felt real handle path and the seen virtual hand, handle, or oar path.
\end{itemize}

These custom items are exploratory thesis-specific measures. They will not be mixed into VEQ Ownership or VEQ Agency scores. Negatively phrased mismatch items will be reported separately unless a final scoring scheme is explicitly defined. This separation is important because a participant may rate the boat-realistic mode as more recognizable rowing while also reporting weaker direct control or stronger handle/oar mismatch.

Comfort, dizziness, nausea, pain, or unsafe discomfort will be recorded through safety checks and debriefing notes. These observations are relevant for feasibility and limitations, but they are not interpreted as embodiment, agency, or sport-action plausibility scores.

After both conditions, participants will answer comparative preference questions. These will ask which representation they preferred overall, which felt more directly connected to the real ergometer movement, and which looked more like rowing on water. Open comments will be collected to identify mismatch sources and details that are not captured by numeric ratings.
