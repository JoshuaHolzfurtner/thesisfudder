\section{Study Design}
\label{sec:study_design_overview}

The study evaluates the XR rowing prototype by comparing two representation modes for the same physical rowing action. In both conditions, participants row on the same ergometer setup. What changes is not the exercise task itself, but the representational relation between the felt ergometer movement and the visible rowing body, handle, oars, and environment.

The first condition is an ergometer-congruent representation. It is designed to preserve close spatial and temporal correspondence between the participant's real interaction with the ergometer and the virtual rowing body. The visible hands, handle relation, seat movement, and body movement are therefore intended to remain as close as possible to the physical setup. This condition prioritizes embodied action congruence: the participant should see a rowing body whose movement appears directly tied to the movement they perform on the machine.

The second condition is a boat-realistic representation. It is designed to make the action legible as rowing on water by showing a rowing body in a boat-like context with virtual oars. This representation is motivated by the fact that ergometer rowing can approximate important parts of rowing movement, but does not reproduce all on-water rowing mechanics, especially oar- and blade-related actions \parencite{lamb1989kinematicComparison,soperHume2004rowingTechnique}. The boat-realistic condition therefore prioritizes sport-action depiction even where the virtual oar path cannot be a literal copy of the real ergometer handle path.

The planned design is within-subjects: every participant will experience both modes. This design is appropriate because the comparison concerns two alternative representations of one physical action, allowing each participant to serve as their own control. The condition order will be counterbalanced where possible to reduce order, learning, adaptation, and fatigue effects.

The first data-collection version does not include physiological threat measures, heart-rate measures, or a hand-offset probe. This keeps the initial evaluation focused on the main representation comparison: whether the two modes differ in reported ownership, agency, embodied action congruence, virtual hand/oar mapping, sport-action plausibility, perceived mismatch, and preference. Comfort and sickness symptoms will be handled as safety and debriefing observations rather than as embodiment outcomes.
