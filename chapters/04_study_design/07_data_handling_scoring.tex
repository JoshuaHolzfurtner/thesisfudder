\section{Data Handling and Scoring}
\label{sec:study_design_data_handling}

Questionnaire responses are recorded per participant and per condition.
The analysis table should include a participant code, condition order, relevant background variables, per-condition ratings, post-comparison answers, and optional open comments.
Condition order must be retained because the within-subject design makes order, learning, novelty, fatigue, and adaptation possible interpretive factors.
Participant names or direct identifiers should not be stored in the analysis table.
If consent forms or contact information must be stored, they should be kept separately from questionnaire responses.

The VEQ Ownership/Acceptance score is calculated as the mean of the selected Ownership/Acceptance items.
The VEQ Control/Agency score is calculated as the mean of the selected Control/Agency items.
These two scores are treated as the primary self-report embodiment outcomes.
If any VEQ item wording is adapted to fit the rowing context, the adaptation must be documented in Appendix~\ref{app:questionnaires} before data collection \parencite{rothLatoschik2020veq}.

Exploratory body-location items are reported separately from the VEQ scores.
They are included because self-location is conceptually relevant to embodiment, but the study does not treat these items as a final VEQ factor score.
The same separation applies to rowing-specific custom items.
Items on handle fit, hand/oar mapping, controllable extension, sport-action plausibility, and mismatch are analyzed as exploratory thesis-specific measures rather than as validated subscales.
If a negative mismatch item is reverse-coded or combined with other items, the scoring rule must be defined before analysis.
Otherwise, mismatch should be reported directly as its own measure.

Preference questions are analyzed only after both conditions have been completed.
They are interpreted as design-facing comparisons, not as embodiment scores.
Open comments are used to contextualize questionnaire patterns, especially when participants describe concrete sources of congruence, mismatch, comfort, or confusion.
If comments are quoted in the thesis, they should be anonymized and checked for identifying information.
If comments are translated or paraphrased, that decision should be documented.

Descriptive statistics should be reported for each condition and measure.
For the primary VEQ outcomes, the analysis should report condition-level Ownership/Acceptance and Control/Agency scores and within-participant differences.
For exploratory measures, the analysis should report direction and pattern rather than overstate scale-level precision.
If the final sample size and response distributions support inferential tests, paired within-subject tests may be used.
If the sample is small, descriptive statistics, within-subject profiles, and open comments should carry the interpretation more strongly than claims of generalizable effects.

Missing data handling must be defined before final analysis.
If an item is skipped, the analysis should state whether the participant's subscale score is omitted, calculated from available items, or marked missing.
The same rule should be applied consistently across conditions.

The data handling approach is intentionally conservative.
It allows the study to identify whether the two representation modes produce different response profiles without implying that one mode is universally superior.
A useful result may be a divergence: for example, one mode may produce stronger reported control, another may be judged more plausible as rowing on water, and preference may follow neither ownership nor agency perfectly.
