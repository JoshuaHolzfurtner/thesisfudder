\section{Embodiment Measures}
\label{sec:study_design_embodiment_measures}

The primary embodiment outcomes are participant-reported ownership and agency.
These outcomes were selected because the study compares two representations of the same real rowing action rather than two unrelated tasks.
The erg-centered mode is designed to keep the visible rowing body close to the participant's real ergometer interaction, while the boat-centered mode may transform the visible hand, handle, and oar movement in order to depict rowing on water.
The questionnaire therefore needs to distinguish whether the virtual rowing body feels like the participant's body from whether the virtual movement feels controlled or caused by the participant's own action \parencite{kilteniEtAl2012senseEmbodiment,kalckertEhrsson2012movingRubberHand}.
\sourcechecknote{This paragraph explains why ownership and agency are measured separately rather than collapsed into one embodiment score.
Kilteni et al. is used as the conceptual source for sense of embodiment in virtual reality, where ownership, agency, and self-location are treated as related but distinguishable components.
Kalckert and Ehrsson is used because their moving rubber-hand work shows that ownership and agency can diverge under movement-based conditions, which is directly relevant to this thesis: one rowing representation may feel controllable without necessarily feeling most like one's own body, or vice versa.
Neither source is rowing-specific.
They justify the construct separation, not any predicted result for the erg-centered or boat-centered mode.}

The planned primary questionnaire basis is the Virtual Embodiment Questionnaire.
For the present study, the relevant primary subscales are Ownership/Acceptance and Control/Agency \parencite{rothLatoschik2020veq}.
Ownership/Acceptance captures whether the virtual body is accepted as body-like and as belonging to the participant.
Control/Agency captures whether the movements of the virtual body are experienced as being controlled, caused, or synchronized with the participant's own movements.
These subscales correspond directly to the thesis comparison because both representation modes show a virtual rowing body, but they differ in how directly that body and its rowing movement correspond to the real ergometer action.
\sourcechecknote{Roth and Latoschik is used here as the primary questionnaire source.
The VEQ provides a validated self-report structure with factors including Ownership/Acceptance, Control/Agency, and Change, with item groups that can be scored as means.
For this thesis, Ownership/Acceptance and Control/Agency are the relevant primary outcomes because the study asks whether the virtual rowing body feels like the participant's body and whether its rowing movement feels controlled or caused by the participant.
The source does not validate the custom rowing-specific items and does not by itself justify treating rowing realism, preference, or comfort as embodiment scores.}

The VEQ Change subscale is not used as a primary outcome in the first evaluation.
This decision follows from the study design: the central manipulation is not avatar body size, body shape, or body-schema change, but the representation of the same physical rowing action as either erg-centered or boat-centered rowing.
The Change items may therefore be omitted or kept outside the primary analysis only if the final questionnaire design explicitly justifies them.

Self-location remains conceptually relevant to the sense of embodiment, but it is treated as exploratory in this study.
The questionnaire therefore includes body-location items that ask whether the participant felt located where the virtual rowing body or virtual hands were seen.
These items are analyzed separately from the VEQ subscales.
This separation is necessary because self-location is conceptually important in embodiment theory, but it is not retained as one of the final VEQ factors used as a primary score in this study \parencite{kilteniEtAl2012senseEmbodiment,gonzalezFrancoPeck2018avatarEmbodiment,rothLatoschik2020veq}.
\sourcechecknote{This paragraph explains the body-location items as exploratory rather than primary VEQ scoring.
Kilteni et al. supports self-location as a component of sense of embodiment.
Gonzalez-Franco and Peck is used because avatar embodiment questionnaire practice includes body-location or co-location item categories.
Roth and Latoschik is used to clarify the VEQ boundary: VEQ is the primary questionnaire basis, but self-location is not treated here as a final VEQ factor score.
Together, these sources justify measuring body-location experience while reporting it separately from VEQ Ownership/Acceptance and Control/Agency.
They do not justify interpreting body-location items as a validated rowing embodiment scale.}

The embodiment measure structure is intentionally profile-based.
Ownership/Acceptance, Control/Agency, and exploratory body-location are reported separately so that the two representation modes can produce different response patterns.
For example, a condition may feel more directly controllable without being preferred overall, or it may look more like rowing on water while still producing stronger handle or hand-position mismatch.
The study therefore does not ask whether one condition is simply "more embodied" in a single global sense.
Instead, it asks which aspects of embodied experience are supported by each representation.
