\section{Ethics, Safety, and Method Limitations}
\label{sec:study_design_ethics_safety_limitations}

The study involves physical exertion on an ergometer while wearing a VR headset. The procedure must therefore include basic safety screening, clear stop criteria, and the option to withdraw at any time without giving a reason. These safety procedures are part of the method because fatigue, discomfort, or headset-related symptoms could affect both participant wellbeing and the interpretation of ratings.

Participants will be informed that they may experience physical fatigue, discomfort, dizziness, or VR-related sickness symptoms. They will be instructed to stop immediately if they feel unsafe or unwell. The experimenter will monitor the session and pause or terminate the trial if necessary.

The first study version deliberately avoids threat stimuli, physiological response measures, and hand-offset probes. This reduces additional ethical complexity and keeps the first evaluation focused on the main representation comparison. If these probes are added in a later study version, they should be described as separate measures with separate justification rather than folded into the current questionnaire design.

The method has expected limitations. It relies mainly on self-report, uses a prototype-specific implementation, and may involve a small or convenience-based sample. The results should therefore be interpreted as evidence about the studied XR rowing setup rather than as general conclusions about all VR rowing systems or all embodiment mechanisms. The final discussion should also distinguish between limitations of the study design and limitations of the prototype implementation, because a low rating may reflect the representation concept, the current calibration/tracking quality, or both.
